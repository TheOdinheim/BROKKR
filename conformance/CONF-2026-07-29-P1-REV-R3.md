## 0. Scope

Reshapes `BoundaryFlow` from an egress-shaped struct into a direction sum type, and places the
Boundary Custody Record, `DestinationClass`, `ContextClass`, and `OriginId`. This makes the inputs a
Phase-6 `Barrier::evaluate` needs **representable** — nothing is evaluated here. Every verdict below
concerns representability, not enforcement.

## 2. Structural invariants

**I-2 (no `Deny → Allow`) — HELD, and unchanged.** `BarrierVerdict` is **not touched** by this
revision. Its `compile_fail,E0599` doctest (`.into_allow()` does not exist) still passes, and for the
same reason — verified by running (`cargo test --doc`, 12 doctests, unchanged). The reshape is to
`BoundaryFlow` (the gate's *input*), not to the verdict.

**I-5 (the spine depends on nothing internal) — HELD.** The new types use only in-crate types
(`crate::crypto::DualSignature`, `crate::ids::{OriginId, Timestamp, DatumRef, …}`,
`crate::classification::Classification`) and `alloc::vec::Vec`. `brokkr-core/Cargo.toml`
`[dependencies]` remains **empty**; `cargo tree` shows no internal or external dependency.

## 3. Representability, stated as such (§7)

This revision's contribution is **representability**, and the record says so plainly rather than
claiming enforcement:

- Before: `BoundaryFlow` was `{ classification, destination }` — egress only. An ingress crossing
  could not be expressed, so `Quarantine` was unreachable from `evaluate`.
- After: `BoundaryFlow` is `Egress { … , bcr }` | `Ingress { datum, bcr, context }`. An ingress flow
  carrying a `Destination`, or an egress flow carrying a `ContextClass`, is **unrepresentable** — the
  bad shapes cannot be built. The gate that *decides* remains Phase 6.

## 4. Backward verdict movement (v1.6 §5.3)

None. No prior record claimed I-9/I-10/I-11 satisfied by the committed types; the Rev 1.5 barrier
surface was a placeholder struct, recorded as such. This revision moves the barrier *input* forward
(representable) without correcting any prior claim downward.
