## 1. AMD-002 — OQGF-P-4 (tolerance grants are scoped, **expiring**, signed)

> *"Each Tolerance Grant … SHALL carry an expiry … Expired or out-of-scope grants SHALL have no
> effect."*

HEIMDALL's `grant_heuristic` checks `self.state().now.0 > grant.expiry.0` (heimdall.rs:268) — the
grant expiry against a **held** clock. A HEIMDALL alive across calls (or whose `set_now` is not
advanced) believes an expired grant is still fresh, so the grant **never expires** and the detector
it suppresses stays silenced. This fails **toward blindness**: a silenced detector produces no
evidence anything is wrong.

**Verdict: PARTIAL — and Phase 8's verdict was wrong at the time (§5.3).** Phase 8
(`CONF-2026-08-12-P8-R1`) recorded P-4 satisfied on a test that injected `now` at construction and
evaluated the grant once — a test that shared the defect's assumption and could not have caught a
held clock. This is the same shape as Phase 4's M-14 correction. P-4 is moved **backward to
PARTIAL**: grants are scoped and signed, but their **expiry is enforced against a held clock**, so
"expired grants SHALL have no effect" is not reliably met. The correction lands with the re-issued
revision (blocked on GAP-2026-08-18-002).

## 2. AMD-005 — OQGF-P-8.5 (fail-safe asymmetry: DAP-confirmed de-escalation)

> *"De-escalation above Baseline SHALL require the Resolution Path criteria to be met and DAP
> confirmation … Where the system is uncertain, it SHALL remain escalated."*

EIR's `resolve` checks the decision expiry `now.0 > d.expiry.0` (eir.rs:242) with `now` read from the
**held** `st.now` (eir.rs:232). The Rev 1.12/1.13 freshness defence (a captured stand-down must not
replay later "when the escalation it clears is real and current") is enforced against a clock the
engine holds — **the defect on the one act that lowers a defence.**

**Verdict: PARTIAL (backward).** The signature and nonce-replay checks are intact; the **expiry**
check reads a held clock, so a stale decision could pass its freshness check on a long-lived EIR. The
DAP-confirmation and raise-only asymmetry hold; the freshness half is held-clock-defeated.

## 3. AMD-005 — OQGF-P-8.3 (hysteresis) and P-8.6 (chronic detection)

> **P-8.3:** dwell/hold before de-escalation. **P-8.6:** an escalation past its declared maximum
> duration SHALL be flagged as chronic.

`may_resolve` (dwell/hold, eir.rs:135–156 via `classify`) and `scan_chronic` (max_duration,
eir.rs:289) both compute elapsed time from a **held** `now` (fed to `classify` from `st.now`, and read
directly in `scan_chronic`). A long-lived EIR believes **no escalation has aged**: hysteresis never
elapses and chronic escalations are never flagged — the opposite failure from over-eager
de-escalation, but still a held-clock defeat of a time-based check.

**Verdict: PARTIAL (backward).** The dwell/hold/max logic is correct **given a correct `now`**; the
`now` is held. Note the **shape trap**: `classify` *takes* a `now` parameter and looks I-13-correct —
only tracing its callers (which feed `st.now`) shows the defect. This is why the closure evidence is
the comparison table, not a grep.

## 4. AMD-001 — OQGF-M-14 (freshness) and the invariant I-13

**I-13 — brokkr-sentinel holds a clock. Verdict: VIOLATED (this crate).** `EirState.now`
(eir.rs:54) and `HeimdallState.now` (heimdall.rs:42) are construction/`set_now` state, and four
decision comparisons read them (table below). I-13 requires the current time as *"a parameter of the
evaluating call, never construction state."* The fix requires `now` on four **core trait methods**
(`ResolutionEngine::{may_resolve, resolve, scan_chronic}`, `ToleranceController::grant_heuristic`),
which is a `brokkr-core` change — **out of scope**, filed as GAP-2026-08-18-002. Compiler-confirmed:
`E0050` (impl vs core-trait arity).
