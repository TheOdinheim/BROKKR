## 1. What I was doing

Implementing OQGF-P-11.2 exactly as Architecture Rev 1.29 (commit `98b20f4`) places it: `FieldName`, a `PurposeFieldPolicy` in `PolicyRegister`, a `fields` declaration on `PersonalDataTag`, a `PurposeFieldResolver` seam, ingress conditions 10 and 11, and `BarrierCondition::PersonalDataFieldOutOfScope`.

Step 0 confirmed the design is **buildable**. Every value the placed rules read has a path from the evaluating code, the seam pattern is committed, and the three missing surfaces are placeable. **The defect is not in the design. It is in one paragraph of the cost analysis, and that paragraph is false.**

## 2. The defect — an assertion about the audit chain that is not true at HEAD

Rev 1.29 §6.5, in *The present field set — declared on the crossing, covered by the BCR signature*:

> *"The cost is real and is stated rather than discovered at implementation. Changing `write_personal` changes `bcr_signed_content`, so every previously issued BCR carrying a personal tag stops verifying. There are none in production today ... A canonical-encoding change is exactly the class of change Rev 1.10 was written about, and **the check that it does not disturb the audit chain is separate: a BCR is not an `AuditRecord`, `record_signed_content` does not encode it, and no `prev` digest covers it.**"*

**The first clause is true. The second and third are false.**

`PersonalDataTag` reaches `record_signed_content` — the chain-linkage digest — by **two** paths, both in `brokkr-audit/src/canonical.rs`:

```
record_signed_content            :699   the bytes every `prev` digests and every GenerationSignature signs
  └─ write_event                 :706
     ├─ AuditEvent::BarrierCrossing        :636
     │    └─ write_flow                    :370
     │         └─ write_personal           :382 (Egress) / :394 (Ingress)
     │              └─ write_personal_tag  :270   ← encodes purpose + retention
     └─ RecordedInput::Shredded            :512
          └─ write_personal_tag            :519   ← the same encoder, second path
```

**`brokkr-audit` keeps its own copy of the personal-tag encoding**, distinct from `brokkr-barrier`'s `write_personal` (`brokkr-barrier/src/canonical.rs:90`). Rev 1.29 named the barrier's copy and did not look for a second one.

**Consequence:** adding `fields` to `PersonalDataTag` changes `write_personal_tag`, hence `write_event`, hence `record_signed_content` — **so it moves the digest of any audit record carrying a `BarrierCrossing` event or a `Shredded` recorded input.** That is precisely the input Rev 1.10 established as the chain-linkage basis, and precisely what the quoted sentence says is untouched.

### 2.1 Why the sentence reads as correct

It answers a real question, accurately, and it is the **wrong question**. *Is a BCR an `AuditRecord`?* No — and that is what the sentence checks. The question that determines the cost is *is a BCR **inside** an `AuditRecord`?* — and it is, because **OQGF-I-13 requires it to be**: *"Every Barrier decision — a crossing allowed, denied, or quarantined, in either direction — SHALL be recorded in Organ 5."* `AuditEvent::BarrierCrossing` (`brokkr-audit/src/event.rs:163`) carries a whole `BoundaryFlow`, personal tag included.

So the architecture's own requirement is what puts the tag in the chain, and the paragraph denying it stands two sections from the requirement that mandates it.

## 3. Root cause — checkable, unchecked, and it is not a §5.4 failure

**This is not a reachability defect and should not be filed as one.** §5.4 asks whether the evaluating code has a path to the value a rule reads. Here every value is reachable, the seam is real, and the design builds. The false claim is not about a rule's inputs; it is about a **consequence** of a type change.

**It is the §10 SLH-DSA class**: an assertion that sounds true, was never verified, and becomes furniture that later revisions carry forward. §10's line said SLH-DSA was "natively available, not yet enabled" and stayed for eighteen revisions after the flag was set. This sentence would have had the same career — it is quotable, it is reassuring, and it is exactly what a future reader assessing a canonical-encoding change would rely on.

**I nearly relied on it myself.** The instruction directed me to *verify* the cost rather than assume it, and without that instruction I would likely have read the paragraph, found `brokkr-barrier`'s `write_personal`, matched it to the sentence, and proceeded. The check that caught it was following the trace one crate further than the sentence pointed.

### 3.1 Would any existing rule have caught it? No.

Stated plainly rather than filled with a proposal:

- **§5.4** governs a placed rule's inputs. This is a claimed consequence, not an input. It would not have fired.
- **§5.3** governs conformance verdicts — implementation, proof, verdict per requirement. The false sentence is a cost note, not a verdict.
- **§7** ("the builder's own summary is not evidence") is the closest in spirit and does not reach it: the claim was made by the DAP in a placed architecture revision, not by the builder in a report.
- **Rev 1.10's discipline** — the linkage digest must not be silently disturbed — is exactly the principle at stake, and it exists as a *design constraint on the encoding*, not as a checklist applied to revisions that touch types feeding it.

**No rule covers "an architecture revision asserts a consequence about committed code."** I am not proposing one to fill the space. The three prior Step-0 stops (GAP-2026-09-07-001, GAP-2026-09-08-001, and this one) were each caught by a builder grounding a placed design before writing code, and that practice — already mandatory — is what caught this. Whether it warrants a rule of its own is a DAP judgment; my observation is only that the existing set does not reach it, and that inventing a rule per incident is how a framework acquires ceremony rather than control.

## 4. Blast radius, measured

- **No persisted chain exists.** `Saga` holds `records: Vec<AuditRecord>` in memory (`brokkr-audit/src/saga.rs:127`); nothing serialises a chain across runs. So no stored record's digest moves.
- **Committed fixtures that construct the affected shapes** build their records in-process, so they move together with the encoding rather than breaking: `brokkr-audit/tests/audit.rs:79` (`RecordedInput::Shredded`) and `:142` (`AuditEvent::BarrierCrossing`); `brokkr-cli/src/lib.rs:685` emits `BarrierCrossing` at runtime; `brokkr-cli/tests/redteam_14c.rs:602` and `redteam_16.rs:602` match on the variant without pinning a digest.
- **Practical cost today: zero. Cost once SAGA persists: not zero**, and the correction matters precisely because the second condition is the one a future reader would be assessing under.

## 5. A second, smaller omission — recorded because the §5.4 table should carry it

Rev 1.29's §5.4 table does not mention that **`BarrierFinding` requires a `classification`** (`brokkr-core/src/barrier.rs`) while **`BoundaryFlow::Ingress` carries none**. Conditions 10 and 11 return `Deny { finding }`, so a finding must be constructible at that point.

**It resolves cleanly and is not a blocker:** conditions 10 and 11 are reached only once provenance is established, which requires a present, matching, verifying, unexpired BCR — and `BoundaryCustodyRecord` carries `classification`. The path is `b.classification`. Recorded here so the table names it rather than leaving the next implementer to rediscover it.

## 6. Disposition — directed by the DAP at filing: accept the encoding change

**Option 1 (adopted).** Accept that the change moves `record_signed_content` for records carrying a `BarrierCrossing` or a `Shredded` input. Zero cost now (in-memory SAGA, no persisted chains); not zero once SAGA persists. The false paragraph goes.

**Option 2 (not available).** Site `fields` where the audit encoding does not reach. Every candidate is on the crossing, and the crossing is what OQGF-I-13 requires to be recorded. There is nowhere to put it that the chain does not see.

**And the property Rev 1.29 wanted is delivered more strongly than it claimed.** The design's stated aim was that the field declaration be signed and tamper-evident. It is covered by the BCR's dual-family signature **and** by the audit chain's hash linkage and generation signatures — two independent attestations, not one. The correction is therefore not a weakening; the revision undersold its own mechanism while misdescribing its cost.

**Labeled per §4.** This disposition **does not unblock me in the convenient direction** — it accepts a broader cost than the placement claimed and requires the implementation to proceed knowing the chain encoding moves. The cheaper outcome would have been to accept the paragraph, implement, and note nothing; the sentence was reassuring and nothing visibly breaks. That route was available and was not taken.
