## 2. The contradiction, stated precisely

§6.9 asserts three things. Any two of them can hold; not all three.

**(a) The chain-linkage digest covers the signatures** — §6.9 "The record" and "The chain":

> *"`prev`: Digest of the PRECEDING record's full canonical bytes, **including its signatures**."*
> (lines 918–919)
>
> *"Each record links to the previous by the digest of its **full canonical bytes including
> signatures**, so an entry cannot be altered after the fact without breaking every link after it.
> Verification walks the chain and recomputes; a mismatch emits the audit-chain-break trigger."*
> (lines 937–939)

**(b) Re-signing appends a signature** — §6.9 "Re-signing", quoting OQGF-A-6:

> *"Re-signing SHALL append a `GenerationSignature` and SHALL NOT replace or remove any earlier one."*
> (line 956) — `AuditRecord.signatures: Vec<GenerationSignature>` (line 927) grows by one.

**(c) Re-signing leaves the chain unchanged** — same section, same requirement:

> *"It also SHALL NOT alter `seq`, `prev`, `at`, `dap`, or `event` — a re-signed record is the same
> record, newly attested, and **the chain through it is unchanged**."* (line 956); OQGF-A-6:
> *"preserving the original signatures **and chain**."*

Put (a) and (b) together: appending a `GenerationSignature` to record *N* changes `N.signatures`, which
changes *N*'s "full canonical bytes including signatures", which changes the digest that *N+1*'s `prev`
was computed from. By (a)'s own words — "an entry cannot be altered after the fact without breaking
every link after it" — re-signing *N* **breaks the link from N+1**. That directly violates (c).

Re-signing is a sanctioned maintenance operation that §6.9 says preserves the chain; under §6.9's own
linkage definition it destroys it. This is the identical failure §6.9 names for erasure —
*"SAGA would report its own compliance action as an integrity attack"* (line 941) — but for erasure the
architecture resolves it (never delete), and for re-signing it does not: (a) and (c) are left standing
side by side, one sentence apart, mutually exclusive.

**The prompt's own Task 1 definition makes the collision explicit.** Task 1 defines the FULL bytes as
"signed content + **all accumulated** signatures" and states "the full bytes are what the next record's
`prev` digests." Under that definition, re-signing changes the full bytes of an already-linked record,
so the next record's `prev` is stale the moment a re-signing occurs. Task 1's definition and Task 4's
preservation requirement are the two horns directly.

**A second, smaller instance of the same shape: `timestamping`.** `AuditRecord.timestamping`
(line 929) is either an RFC-3161 `Token` over the record's canonical content or `Unavailable`. A token
is computed *over* the signed content and attached *after* signing (a TSA stamps the bytes it is given).
If "full canonical bytes" also includes `timestamping`, then attaching the first timestamp — not only a
later re-signing — mutates the full bytes of a record after it may already be someone's `prev`. This
reinforces that post-hoc attachments (the signature set, the timestamp token) do not belong in the
linkage digest; but §6.9 as written puts the signature set in it.

## 3. The two resolutions available to me, and why §4 forbids me adopting either

**Resolution B — linkage digest over SIGNED CONTENT only (exclude `signatures`, exclude
`timestamping`).** This resolves the contradiction cleanly and is, on the merits, defensible: the
linkage digest would cover the immutable header and event (`seq`, `prev`, `at`, `dap`, `event`), so any
tampering with those breaks the chain (tamper-evidence preserved); and each `GenerationSignature` is
verified **independently** against that same signed content (signature integrity preserved by a separate
check, not by the linkage digest). Re-signing appends signatures that the linkage digest never covered,
so the chain is preserved. **But this contradicts §6.9's explicit words** — "full canonical bytes
**including signatures**." Adopting it silently would be (i) building something that contradicts the
read-only architecture I am directed to build *from*, and (ii) choosing the reading that unblocks me.
CLAUDE.md §1 and §4 both forbid this: *"If the architecture is wrong or incomplete, that is an
amendment-gap report, not a code decision you make silently,"* and *"Never invent a rule to fill a
framework gap."*

**Resolution C — freeze the linkage digest at seal time (signed content + the signatures present when
the record became a `prev` = the original signature only), and define verification to recompute over the
original signature only, not the current set.** This makes all three of §6.9's statements literally
true: the digest "includes signatures" (the original one), and later re-signings append signatures
outside the frozen prefix, so the chain is unchanged. **But §6.9 nowhere states this rule.** It says
verification "recomputes" the digest of "full canonical bytes including signatures" — a faithful
implementer digests the record's *current* `signatures` vec (all of them) and the chain breaks after the
first re-signing. To make C work I must author the unstated convention "linkage and verification use the
signed content plus the first signature only." That is inventing the governing scheme — the precise
"convention about what the bytes mean" hazard §4 reserves to the architecture and the DAP.

Both resolutions are, in the prompt's word, "defensible." Neither is mine to adopt: one contradicts the
committed spec text, the other invents a rule the spec does not carry. Per Task 4's own instruction and
CLAUDE.md §4, the disposition is to STOP and report.

## 4. Why the whole phase stops (not "build the buildable tasks and gap the chain")

The chain-linkage digest is not a corner of Phase 7 — it **is** the Audit Spine. Task 1 (canonical: what
the FULL bytes are and what `prev` covers), Task 3 (the chain: `prev` = digest of the previous record's
full bytes; verify by recompute; emit the A.6.1 Signal on mismatch), and Task 4 (re-signing preserves
the chain) all turn on the single definition this gap is about. Task 5's load-bearing test —
`test_oqgf_p_11_5_erasure_preserves_chain` — and Task 4's most important test —
`test_oqgf_p_11_7_resigned_erased_record_stays_irrecoverable`, which re-signs and re-verifies the chain —
cannot be written, let alone pass, until "does the chain survive a signature append" has a defined
answer. Building the record/event types (Task 2) and the registers (Task 7) now would be rework once the
linkage definition lands, because the record's canonical encoding is exactly what changes. So the phase
stops cold, as Phases 4, 5, and 6 did at their Step-0 checks, pending an architecture revision.

## 6. Recommendation (a recommendation only — I do not adopt it)

Placed by the DAP as an architecture revision to §6.9 (never me), committed before Phase 7 builds under
it (§5.2). Any drafted amendment may only add or tighten; none of the options below relaxes a
requirement — each **preserves** both tamper-evidence and re-signing, differing only in where signature
integrity is checked.

- **(Preferred) Define the chain-linkage digest over the record's SIGNED CONTENT only** — `seq`, `prev`,
  `at`, `dap`, `event` — excluding the accumulating `signatures` set and the post-hoc `timestamping`,
  with **each** `GenerationSignature` verified independently against that signed content and the
  timestamp token verified against it too. Correct §6.9's "full canonical bytes including signatures" to
  "over the record's signed content; the signature set and timestamp token are verified independently,
  not folded into the linkage digest." This is the only reading that is self-consistent **without** an
  implicit "first-signature-only" convention: tamper-evidence of the immutable header/event is preserved
  (any change to signed content breaks the chain), re-signing is preserved (signatures were never in the
  linkage digest), and signature integrity is preserved (the per-signature check that must happen
  anyway). It relocates signature integrity from the linkage digest to the independent verification —
  relaxing nothing.

- **(Alternative) Freeze the linkage digest at seal time** — signed content plus the signatures present
  when the record became a `prev` (the original `GenerationSignature`) — and state in §6.9 that
  verification recomputes over the original signature only, not the current set. Honors "including
  signatures" literally, but requires §6.9 to carry the freeze rule explicitly (Resolution C, made a
  spec statement rather than a builder invention).

- **(Alternative) Redefine re-signing** so it does not mutate a linked record — e.g., a separate
  re-signature side-log keyed by `seq`, leaving `AuditRecord` bytes immutable after seal. Preserves the
  literal linkage-over-full-bytes reading at the cost of moving signatures out of the record. Heaviest
  change; noted for completeness.

**Labeling per §4 (does any recommendation unblock me?): yes — all of them do, and I say so plainly.**
Any precise definition of the linkage digest, whichever the DAP picks, lets the phase proceed; there is
no disposition of this gap that leaves me blocked. So unlike prior gaps — where one option among several
was the unblocking one — here the *unblock* is intrinsic to resolving the contradiction at all, and I
flag that the coincidence of my interest and every option is total. My genuine engineering preference is
the first (signed-content linkage), because it is the only option that is internally consistent without
an unstated convention and because signature integrity is already independently checked; I would prefer
it even though a stricter-looking option (freeze, or side-log) exists, so the preference is not the
lazier one. I adopt none of them. The choice is the DAP's, placed as a §6.9 revision.
