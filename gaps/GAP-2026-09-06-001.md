## 1. What I was doing

AMD-018 (Key Custody Tier Resolution, commit `cbe0645`) resolved the OQGF-R-6 contradiction this
repository referred upward at Architecture Rev 1.2 and carried for eighteen revisions. §A.4.3 had
stated R-6 as an unqualified SHALL requiring 3-of-5 threshold custody at every level; §A.4.4's
conformance table placed threshold custody at High-Assurance only. AMD-018 makes the tiered reading
govern and places a substantive requirement at Enhanced where there had been none.

AMD-018 §AMD.4 requires that a prior R-6 verdict not be carried forward unexamined, and states that
the amendment "does not automatically improve any verdict; it makes each verdict determinable." I was
drafting Architecture Rev 1.20 to record the amendment, and the drafting instruction was explicit:
verify against §6.11 and the CBOM section before asserting satisfaction, and do not claim satisfied on
the amendment's say-so.

I determined the verdict. It is **ABSENT**.

## 2. The finding — OQGF-R-6.2 is ABSENT at the declared tier

BROKKR declares **Enhanced (OQGF-E)**, so **R-6.2 binds**:

> *"Long-lived secrets SHALL be held in a hardware security module or equivalent hardware-backed key
> store from which the private key material cannot be extracted, and key issuance and rotation SHALL
> require dual control — no single individual or credential SHALL be sufficient to issue, rotate, or
> authorize use of a long-lived secret. The custody model, the hardware boundary, and the dual-control
> procedure SHALL be declared in the CBOM."*
> — AMD-018 §AMD.2.1

Three elements. Each was checked separately against committed source rather than against the
architecture's description of itself.

| # | Element | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hardware boundary** — private key material non-extractable from an HSM or equivalent | **ABSENT** | No HSM, PKCS#11, or `cryptoki` integration exists anywhere in the workspace (grep across all `.rs` and `.toml`, `target/` excluded: zero hits). Long-lived keys are generated **in software** by `DualKeyPair::generate()` (`brokkr-crypto/src/sign.rs:120`) → `ffi::MlDsa65::generate()` / `ffi::SlhDsaShake192s::generate()` — wolfCrypt software keygen — invoked for all four long-lived keypairs (principal, SAGA, HEIMDALL, barrier) at `brokkr-cli/src/main.rs:335–338`. SAGA holds its signer as `Mutex<DualKeyPair>` **in process memory** (`brokkr-audit/src/saga.rs:145`). AMD-018 §A.4.5's Enhanced test — *"request an export and confirm refusal"* — has no boundary that could refuse it. |
| 2 | **Dual control** — no single individual or credential sufficient to issue or rotate | **ABSENT** | No dual-control procedure, two-party authorization, or quorum mechanism exists in the specification or in the code. A single operator invoking the CLI generates every long-lived key unilaterally. AMD-018 §A.4.5's Enhanced test — *"attempt a single-operator issuance and confirm it is refused"* — would be a single-operator issuance that **succeeds**. |
| 3 | **CBOM declaration** — custody model, hardware boundary, and dual-control procedure declared | **ABSENT** | `Cbom` (`brokkr-core/src/genome.rs:79–87`) carries `cyclonedx: String`, `algorithms: Vec<AlgorithmId>`, and `signature: DualSignature`. **There is no custody field.** Architecture Rev 1.2–1.19 §1.4 cited §6.11 for the declaration and §6.11 cited the CBOM — a circular reference in which neither names a field, a schema, or a mechanism. Committed fixtures put `"<cbom/>"` in the `cyclonedx` string. |

**`Partial` is not available.** `Partial` means some element of a requirement is satisfied and another
is not. Here **no element is satisfied**, so the honest verdict is `absent`. I record the stricter
verdict deliberately: the drafting instruction anticipated one missing element and asked for `partial`
naming it, and I depart from that only because three are missing rather than one. Recording `absent`
relaxes nothing, which is why I take it without waiting — but the classification is the DAP's to
overrule if a deployment-supplied HSM is intended to count (see §9).

## 3. BROKKR meets no tier of OQGF-R-6, including Baseline

R-6.1 (Baseline) permits software-held keys — but conditionally:

> *"Long-lived secrets SHALL be protected against extraction, and the protection mechanism SHALL be
> declared. **Software-held keys are permitted at Baseline if the CBOM declares them as such.**"*
> — AMD-018 §AMD.2.1

BROKKR's keys are software-held, which is R-6.1 territory. **R-6.1 is not satisfied either**, because
the permission is conditioned on a CBOM declaration and no declaration mechanism exists — the same
missing field as element 3 above. There is no tier of R-6 that BROKKR's current key custody meets.

This is recorded because it changes the shape of the remedy. If the gap were only "Enhanced is
ambitious," a tier discussion would be available. It is not: the declaration obligation binds at every
tier, and it is unmet at every tier.

## 4. The second finding — an unevidenced security claim survived eighteen revisions

This is the part of the finding I consider more serious than the unmet requirement, and it is why this
report is filed rather than folded into a conformance record.

From Architecture Rev 1.2 through Rev 1.19, §1.4 and §6.11 stated:

> *"BROKKR's long-lived signing keys — REGIN's genome key and SAGA's audit key — are **HSM-backed with
> dual-control issuance**, and their custody model is **declared in the CBOM** (§6.11)."*

**No part of that sentence was true**, and none of it was ever implemented, specified, or evidenced. It
was struck at Rev 1.20 (commit `3f9805d`) and retained under strikethrough per §8 as the record of what
was claimed.

CLAUDE.md §7 forbids exactly this construction: *"Your own summary is not evidence... Every claim in
every report SHALL point at something the DAP can independently open and read."* The claim pointed at
§6.11; §6.11 pointed back at the CBOM; the CBOM had no field. **A claim that terminates in a loop
points at nothing**, and the loop is what made it survivable — each half looked as though the other half
carried the evidence.

Two aggravating facts, recorded plainly rather than softened:

- It is a claim about **the system's own security posture**, in **the document that governs the build**.
- It survived **eighteen architecture revisions** and a **sixteen-phase adversarial campaign**
  (`reports/REDTEAM-13A` … `REDTEAM-16`, four hardening passes, two security reviews) that found
  thirty-eight findings and zero CRITICAL/HIGH.

## 5. Why it survived — a named gap standing in front of an unnamed one

The mechanism is worth recording because it generalizes.

Every review that reached OQGF-R-6 encountered, first, the **tier ambiguity**: an unqualified SHALL in
§A.4.3 against a High-Assurance-only conformance table in §A.4.4. The architecture's handling of it was
correct and conspicuously principled — recorded PARTIAL, referred upward, with an explicit refusal to
resolve a framework contradiction in its own favor:

> *"Naming an unmet requirement is conformant. Quietly reading it down to a tier where it vanishes is
> not — and reading it down would have been the disposition that unblocked the build, which is precisely
> why it was not chosen."*

That paragraph **reads as rigor and closes the question.** A reviewer arriving at R-6 found a gap already
named, already reasoned about, and already escalated — and stopped there. Nobody asked whether the
*interim posture* named in the very next sentence existed, because the referral was the interesting
sentence and the interim posture was the throwaway one.

**A prominently named gap can conceal a second gap standing directly behind it, and the second one is
the one nobody is looking for.** The concealment was not deliberate and the referral was not wrong; the
referral was the best available handling of a real contradiction. But it functioned as cover, and it
functioned as cover *for eighteen revisions* precisely because it was well-reasoned.

AMD-018 removed the ambiguity. Removing it removed the cover, and what was behind it became visible on
the first check that had to look past the referral to reach a verdict.

**Two further observations bear on how this class is caught in future:**

- The false claim lived in **the architecture**, and the architecture is what phase checks are checked
  *against*, not checked. §5.3 requires enumerating requirements from the corpus and testing the
  architecture against them — which is exactly what caught this, four years of revisions late, the first
  time a corpus requirement was specific enough to name the three elements to look for.
- The 13A–16 red-team campaign was scoped to attacking **the implementation**. `REDTEAM-15A` F-23 and
  F-24 came close — F-24 records *"no signed REGIN genome wired into orchestrator (deployment gap)"* —
  but no phase was tasked with asking whether the architecture's factual assertions about the deployment
  were true. **An adversarial campaign against the code cannot find a false sentence in the spec.**

## 6. Consequence — a documented compensating control does not exist

`reports/SECURITY-REVIEW-2026-08-30-R1.md` records finding **M-3** (the SAGA hash chain covers signed
content, not the signature, so a key-holding insider can modify and re-sign undetected). It was
dispositioned as an accepted architectural trust boundary rather than a code defect, and the acceptance
rests on a stated compensating control:

> *"**Key custody** — OQGF-R-6 (threshold / HSM custody of the audit-signing key)... A key held under
> 3-of-5 threshold or **in an HSM with dual-control issuance** denies any single insider the unilateral
> rewrite M-3 describes."*

**BROKKR has neither.** The audit-signing key is generated in software and held in process memory
(`brokkr-audit/src/saga.rs:145`). M-3's acceptance therefore currently rests on a control that does not
exist, and the same applies to the custody mitigation cited for F-30 in
`reports/SECURITY-BOUNDARIES-2026-08-30-R1.md`.

This does not make M-3 a code defect — the disposition's reasoning about the design is unaffected. It
makes M-3 **an accepted risk whose stated mitigation is absent**, which is a different and worse
position than the one the security review recorded, and the DAP should know that the acceptance was
made on that basis.

## 7. What this blocks, and what it does not

Stated precisely, because §5.3's "the build stops" needs an object and there is no phase in flight.

**Blocked:**

- **Any claim of Enhanced (OQGF-E) conformance.** R-6.2 binds at the declared tier and is `absent`;
  §A.0.6 forbids claiming a level higher than the lowest-level organ implemented. Architecture Rev 1.20
  §1.5 already records Enhanced conformance as NOT CLAIMED, so this changes no public claim — it
  supplies the strongest current reason for the existing one.
- **Any production deployment or promotion to a regulated environment**, on the plain reading of §A.4.4
  as amended.
- **High-Assurance readiness in any form.** R-6.3 is defined as R-6.2 *plus* threshold custody, so it is
  not independently reachable.

**Not blocked:**

- No phase is in flight; there is no in-progress build to halt, and I have written no code under this
  finding.
- Continued development, testing, and red-teaming against the current tree. The finding is a custody gap
  in the deployment posture, not a defect in the spine's logic, and nothing in the governed action cycle
  depends on it being closed to be correct.

I record this distinction rather than declaring a blanket stop because a stop with no object is not
enforceable, and an unenforceable stop is the kind of statement that gets ignored the second time.

## 8. Closure scope — scope, not a schedule

Recorded so the gap is bounded. **Scheduling is the DAP's and is not proposed here.**

1. **A hardware boundary.** An HSM or equivalent hardware-backed key store — PKCS#11 (`cryptoki`) or
   equivalent — from which private key material cannot be extracted. Note the dependency direction: this
   is a `brokkr-crypto` surface (a key *handle* abstraction rather than `DualKeyPair`'s owned key bytes),
   and every governance crate that signs consumes it. It is predominantly deployment work with a
   `brokkr-crypto` seam.
2. **A dual-control issuance procedure**, genuinely two-party for issuance and rotation. This is an
   operational and organizational control. AMD-018 §AMD.2.1 governs how it may be described: *"a declared
   custody model that overstates the separation actually achieved is a conformance failure, not a
   documentation defect."*
3. **A CBOM custody field with a schema**, in the typed half of `Cbom` rather than the opaque CycloneDX
   string — for the OQGF-G-5 reason every other gate-evaluated fact is typed, and because §6.2 is explicit
   that the promotion gate evaluates the typed inventory and not the document.

**Item 3 is the only one that is code, and it is the least of the three.** A required custody field makes
a *declaration* structural; it does not make the declaration *true*. A CBOM asserting "HSM-backed, dual
control" over software keys held in process memory would **satisfy the field and fail the requirement** —
which is precisely the substance-versus-mechanism failure AMD-018 §AMD.2.1 legislates against. Items 1
and 2 are the requirement; item 3 records it.

I flag this explicitly because item 3 is the tempting one: it is tractable, it is in my scope to build,
and it would produce a visible green artifact. **It would also produce a genome that declares a custody
posture BROKKR does not have**, which is worse than the current state, where the absence is at least
honest.

## 9. Recommendation (a recommendation only — I adopt none)

Placed by the DAP, never by me (§0, §1).

- **(A — preferred) Accept the `ABSENT` verdict as recorded in Rev 1.20, and treat R-6.2 closure as
  deployment work with a `brokkr-crypto` seam.** The verdict is already placed in Rev 1.20 §1.4, §6.11,
  §13, §14 (commit `3f9805d`); this recommendation is that it stand rather than be revisited toward
  `partial`.

- **(B — additive, and NOT a substitute for A) Place a typed CBOM custody field as a seventh predicate of
  the OQGF-G-4 promotion gate** (BROKKR-ARCH §6.2), so a genome whose declared custody model is absent or
  malformed fails promotion. This is an architecture change and therefore the DAP's to place; the builder
  proposes and does not draft it. **It would not by itself move R-6.2 off `ABSENT`**, and it should not be
  taken as progress toward conformance — see §8.

- **(C) Record a Risk Register entry (OQGF-P-10 / AMD-008).** R-6.2 `ABSENT` is a risk in scope: it is
  identified, unowned, and undispositioned, and OQGF-P-10.1 requires every identified risk to carry a
  description, an assessment, a named DAP owner, and exactly one disposition. Suggested as
  **RISK-2026-0008**, following the RISK-2026-0007 precedent of a recommended-but-not-placed entry. I do
  not propose the disposition — `Accept` under OQGF-P-9 with a scope and expiry, or `Reduce` with a
  tracked plan, is a governance judgment, and AMD-018 §AMD.1.1's warning about requirements that
  implementers cannot honestly satisfy is relevant to it.

- **(D) Re-examine M-3's acceptance** in `reports/SECURITY-REVIEW-2026-08-30-R1.md`, whose stated
  compensating control does not exist (§6). Whether the acceptance stands on other grounds is the DAP's
  call; it should be made knowingly.

**Labeling per CLAUDE.md §4 — does any recommendation unblock me?**

**Not in the ordinary sense: I am not blocked.** No phase is in flight, and the ABSENT verdict is already
placed in a committed revision. Recommendation (B) creates work for me rather than removing it, and (C)
and (D) create work for others.

**But there is a coincidence of interest here, and the rule exists to make me name it rather than to
make it disqualifying.** My framing throughout — in Rev 1.20 §6.11 and again in §8 above — is that *two
of R-6.2's three elements are not code, and the third is the least of the three*. That framing is, I
believe, correct, and AMD-018's own assessment procedures support it: the hardware boundary is tested by
requesting an export from a live system, and dual control by attempting a single-operator issuance in an
organization. Neither is a compiler's or a test's to check.

**It is also the framing that assigns the builder the least work and the DAP's organization the most.**
A different disposition — "build the key-handle abstraction and the quorum mechanism, and treat the
deployment as configuration" — would put substantially more of the closure inside my scope. I do not
believe that reading is correct, and I am not recommending it. **I am recording that my analysis and my
convenience point the same direction**, so the DAP weighs the recommendation knowing that. Per §4, naming
the coincidence is the point.

One further self-check, recorded because it is the disposition I most had to refuse: **`satisfied` was
available on paper.** AMD-018 places R-6.2 at Enhanced, BROKKR declares Enhanced, and the architecture
already asserted the posture R-6.2 describes — recording `satisfied` would have required only trusting
the document's own prior sentence. That is the circular audit §5.3 names, and the sentence being trusted
is the one that turns out to be false.
