## 1. What I was doing

Building SINDRI step 1 (Signal 1, identity, OQGF-M-1). The phase instruction says:

> "Verify the attestation's own signatures against the resolved key — the attestation carries
> `signatures: DualSignature`; check them with DualPublicKey::verify_dual over the attestation's
> canonical content. **If defining that canonical content is non-trivial or ambiguous from the
> committed types, STOP and report it as a gap rather than inventing an encoding.**"

I checked. Defining that canonical content **is** ambiguous from the committed types.

## 2. What the committed types do not resolve

- `Attestation` is `{ subject: SubjectId, measurements: Digest, freshness: Nonce, signatures:
  DualSignature }` — `brokkr-core/src/crypto.rs:71-77`. Its own doc comment says: *"Opaque
  payload; verification is a Phase 2/4 concern. No crypto is performed here."* The committed
  types **do not define what `signatures` signs over** — there is no canonical "signed content
  of an attestation."
- The only encoding of an `Attestation` anywhere in the tree is
  `brokkr-intent/src/canonical.rs:123-128` (`write_attestation`), and it is **(a) private** to
  `brokkr-intent` and **(b) includes `a.signatures` in the bytes**. That is the encoding an
  *entry* uses to commit to its `hop_identity` inside `entry_signed_content`
  (`canonical.rs:141-148`) — i.e. it is what the *hop's* signature covers, not what the
  attestation's *own* `signatures` field covers. Reusing it for attestation self-verification
  is circular (the signature would have to cover itself).
- Therefore, to "verify the attestation's own signatures" I would have to **invent** a new
  canonical encoding of the attestation's signed content (e.g. `subject || measurements ||
  freshness`). Inventing a signed-content encoding is exactly what §4 and the instruction
  forbid, and it would bind whatever component eventually issues attestations (attestation
  issuance is not yet built — Option A key resolution and the issuer are later phases).

## 3. Why this is secondary, not phase-halting on its own

Unlike GAP-2026-07-24-001, Signal 1 has a **non-inventing construction** that is
cryptographically meaningful with the committed types:

- `resolve(identity)` → `None` ⇒ the subject is not a declared root of trust ⇒
  `AnergyReason::IdentityUnverified` (this is the M-11 anergy property the phase's
  `test_oqgf_m_11_identity_unverified` targets, and it is fully buildable).
- `resolve(identity)` → `Some(key)` ⇒ the subject **is** a declared root of trust. Proof that
  the presenter actually controls that identity's key comes from **Signal 2**: SINDRI binds
  `identity.subject` to the chain's current hop and `verify_chain_public` verifies that hop's
  **entry signature** under `resolve(hop.subject)`. The chain-entry signature is the
  proof-of-possession; the attestation's *own* signature is redundant with it in this
  architecture.

So Signal 1 can be built as **"identity resolves to a declared root **and** binds to the
chain's verified current hop"** without any invented attestation encoding. This gap only
blocks the *additional, redundant* intrinsic-signature check the instruction asked for.

Because GAP-2026-07-24-001 halts the phase regardless, I am **not** building this workaround
now; I record it so the DAP can dispose of both gaps together.

## 4. Recommendation (a recommendation only — I do not adopt it)

- **Preferred:** at Phase 4, define Signal 1 as *resolve-to-declared-root + bind
  `identity.subject` to the chain's current hop* (above), and **drop the intrinsic
  attestation-signature check** as redundant with the Signal-2 entry signature. This is not a
  relaxation: the actor's control of its key is still cryptographically proven, by Signal 2.
  **Labeling per §4: this option unblocks the Signal-1 step** (it lets me build it without the
  missing encoding). I flag it. It removes a *redundant* check, not a load-bearing one — the
  proof-of-possession is preserved in Signal 2 — but the judgment that it is redundant is the
  DAP's to ratify, not mine to assume.
- **Alternative:** if the DAP wants an intrinsic attestation signature verified, then the
  **attestation's canonical signed-content must be defined in the committed types** (in
  `brokkr-core`, where `Attestation` lives, or a shared canonical module), placed by the DAP as
  a core revision and committed before Phase 4 builds under it. SINDRI would then verify it via
  `DualPublicKey::verify_dual` over that committed encoding. I must not author that encoding.

## 5. Interaction with M-8 / Option B

The `KeyResolver` seam (Option B `RegistryResolver`) is unaffected by this gap: it resolves a
`SubjectId` to a `DualPublicKey` regardless of how Signal 1 uses the result. Both gaps are
about *what SINDRI checks*, not *where keys come from*.
