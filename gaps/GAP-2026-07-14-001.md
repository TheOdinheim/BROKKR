## 3. Why I cannot proceed without inventing something

To carry these into Phase 1 I would have to decide, for each, either that it does not apply
to a coding agent (declare `n.a.` on the DAP's behalf) or how BROKKR satisfies it (invent a
hook the architecture does not contain). Both are the builder placing a decision that
CLAUDE.md Section 1 reserves to the architecture and the DAP: *"You do not decide what BROKKR
should be,"* and *"the builder must never be the channel by which its own governing
specification changes"* (Section 0). Several of these are *plausibly* `n.a.` for a coding
agent — but "plausibly n.a. and undeclared" is exactly the silently-missing case Section 5.3
names, and adopting the plausible reading myself is the Section-4-forbidden move of choosing
what unblocks me.

## 4. Recommendation (a recommendation only — I do not adopt it)

I recommend the DAP dispose of each finding by one of three routes, and place the result as
a revision of the architecture (never me):

- **Declare `n.a.` in Section 1.4 with justification** — most likely correct for I-1, I-2,
  I-5 (HNDL TLS-monitoring is a network-appliance function, arguably not a coding agent's),
  M-6 (fold into the R-2 model-provider reinterpretation), and A.6.1 (an operations
  document, not a property of the build artifact).
- **Add a BROKKR hook** — most likely correct for G-7 (a key-lifecycle note in the crypto
  section / Phase 2), M-5 (state the mutual-auth posture for the reasoner-API channel), and
  R-6 (state signing-key custody, or declare `n.a.` at Enhanced as HA-tier).
- **Defer with a dated marker** — for any the DAP judges real but out of scope for the
  current phases, recorded as a deferral rather than left silent.

This gap **adds** clarity and **relaxes nothing**. No requirement is weakened by resolving
it; each disposition either binds a hook or records an explicit inapplicability.

## SUBSEQUENT FINDING — 7 September 2026 (annotation per §8; the body and the disposition above are unchanged)

**Directed by:** Jeremy Rose, DAP. **This section records that one disposition above did not hold, and
why the failure stayed invisible across eighteen architecture revisions.**

**This report's OQGF-R-6 finding was correct.** Section 2's table recorded it as *"Undeclared; HA-tier
in the conformance table but the SHALL is general and the architecture is silent"* — an ABSENT
verdict on the custody of BROKKR's long-lived signing keys.

**Rev 1.2's closure of it rested on a sentence that was never true.** The disposition had two halves.
The first — referring the §A.4.3-versus-§A.4.4 tier contradiction upward as a framework ambiguity —
was correct, and it was eventually answered by **AMD-018** (commit `cbe0645`), which tiered R-6 into
R-6.1, R-6.2, and R-6.3. The second half was an interim posture:

> *"BROKKR's long-lived signing keys — REGIN's genome key and SAGA's audit key — are **HSM-backed with
> dual-control issuance**, and their custody model is **declared in the CBOM**."*

**No part of that was ever implemented, specified, or evidenced.** There is no HSM, PKCS#11, or
`cryptoki` integration anywhere in the workspace; keys are generated in software by
`DualKeyPair::generate()` and held in process memory; there is no dual-control procedure; and `Cbom`
had no custody field, so the claim that the model was "declared in the CBOM" pointed at §6.11, which
pointed back at the CBOM. **A claim that terminates in a loop points at nothing.** The full
element-by-element verification is GAP-2026-09-06-001 §2; the claim was struck from the architecture
at **Rev 1.20** (commit `3f9805d`) and retained there under strikethrough.

**The original gap was not wrong. Its closure was.** That distinction is the reason this annotation
exists. The Phase 0.5 conformance check found a real absence, said so, and stopped the build; the
disposition that reopened the build did so partly on an assertion with nothing behind it. Fifty-four
days and eighteen architecture revisions later the same requirement returned to the same verdict by a
different route — **`ABSENT`, recorded at Rev 1.20 §14** — which is where it stands today.

**Why it stayed invisible, which is the part worth carrying forward.** Every review that reached
OQGF-R-6 met the *tier ambiguity* first, handled conspicuously well: recorded PARTIAL, referred
upward, with an explicit refusal to resolve a framework contradiction in the architecture's own
favour. That paragraph reads as rigor and closes the question. Nobody asked whether the interim
posture named in the very next sentence existed, because the referral was the interesting sentence
and the posture was the throwaway one. **A prominently named gap can conceal a second gap standing
directly behind it, and the second one is the one nobody is looking for.** AMD-018 removed the
ambiguity, and removing it removed the cover.

**Current state of the eight findings.** Seven remain disposed as Rev 1.2 recorded. **OQGF-R-6 is
open**: R-6.2 is `ABSENT` at the declared Enhanced tier, with the hardware boundary and the
dual-control procedure unbuilt and the CBOM-declaration element implemented at commits `a8900b8` and
`da3543b` (ARCH Rev 1.21/1.22). Closing the other two elements is deployment work. The live record is
GAP-2026-09-06-001 and BROKKR-ARCH §13/§14, not this file.
