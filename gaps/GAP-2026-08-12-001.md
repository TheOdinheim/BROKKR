## 2. The blocker — `ResolveError` has no variant for an expired or replayed decision

§6.8 (Rev 1.12) requires:

> *"EIR SHALL refuse a decision whose `now > expiry`, and SHALL refuse a `nonce` it has already accepted
> for that escalation."*

`ResolutionEngine::resolve` returns `Result<ReturnedToBaseline, ResolveError>`, so a **refusal is an
`Err(ResolveError)`**. The committed `ResolveError` (`brokkr-core/src/resolution.rs`, **unchanged** by
Rev 1.12) has exactly three variants:

| Variant | Meaning (from its message) |
|---|---|
| `CriteriaNotMet` | *"resolution criteria not met (OQGF-P-8.1)"* — the **clear condition** |
| `HysteresisNotSatisfied` | *"dwell/hold hysteresis not satisfied (OQGF-P-8.3)"* — **dwell/hold** timing |
| `NeedsDapConfirmation` | *"de-escalation above baseline requires DAP confirmation (OQGF-P-8.5)"* — **missing DAP** |

**None represents "the decision is expired" or "the nonce was replayed."** Each of the three carries a
distinct, corpus-grounded meaning that a freshness/replay refusal would misstate:

- Reusing **`NeedsDapConfirmation`** is the worst fit and the most misleading: a replayed decision **was**
  DAP-confirmed — it carries a valid DAP signature; it is *stale* or *being replayed*, not *unconfirmed*.
  Reporting "needs DAP confirmation" for a replay attack tells an investigator the opposite of what
  happened.
- Reusing **`CriteriaNotMet`** misreports a freshness failure as a clear-condition (P-8.1) failure.
- Reusing **`HysteresisNotSatisfied`** misreports it as a dwell/hold (P-8.3) failure.

This is the **exact** imprecision Rev 1.12 corrected for `ToleranceError`: Phase 8 had refused a forged
grant as `OutOfScope` because `ToleranceError` had no `SignatureInvalid` variant — *"fail-closed, but
untrue in the record, in exactly the place someone investigating an attack would be reading"* (§6.7). Rev
1.12 added `SignatureInvalid`. **The symmetric variants for `ResolveError` were not added** — an asymmetry
this Step-0 check surfaces. Task 2 anticipates it verbatim.

## 3. Why I cannot resolve it within scope

Adding variants to `ResolveError` is a change to **`brokkr-core`**, and this revision's scope is *"Touch
`brokkr-sentinel` and `brokkr-audit` ONLY. Do NOT modify `brokkr-core`."* The only in-scope ways to
"enforce" freshness would be to (a) reuse a wrong variant — forbidden by Task 2 and by Rev 1.12's own
principle — or (b) not return an `Err` at all, which `resolve`'s signature does not permit and which would
fail to *refuse*. Neither is acceptable, so the revision stops.

**The core–sentinel encoding disagreement Task 1 exists to close (reported per Task 1's instruction):** the
sentinel-local `resolution_signed_content` uses tag `brokkr-sentinel:resolution:v1` and encodes `esc,
cleared, dap(name,id), at` — **no `nonce`, no `expiry`** (the fields did not exist at Phase 8). Core's uses
tag `brokkr-core:resolution:v1` and encodes those two fields as well. So for the same decision the two
produce **different bytes**, on two counts (the tag, and the two missing fields). Phase 8 was internally
consistent (the sentinel signed and verified with its own encoding), but a DAP tool signing via core would
now be rejected by EIR — which is precisely the "field-order disagreement rejects legitimate decisions while
looking exactly like an attack" hazard, and precisely why Task 1 moves both parties onto core's one
definition.

## 5. Why the whole revision stops (not "build 1, 3, 4 and gap 2")

Building Tasks 1/3/4 and the arity fix would make the workspace **compile and go green** — while
`nonce`/`expiry` are present and signed over but **not enforced**. That is a green build that silently lacks
the replay protection the fields exist to provide, in the one act in BROKKR that *lowers* a defense. Task 5
requires `test_oqgf_p_8_5_replayed_nonce_is_refused` — *"THE REPLAY TEST — this is why the fields exist"* —
to pass, which is impossible without Task 2. A revision that shipped freshness fields with no enforcement,
and could not write its own load-bearing test, would be a false completion. So the revision stops as a
whole, pending the core variant, exactly as Phases 4/5/6/7 stopped at Step 0 on a missing core surface. The
workspace remains red (it has been since the core Rev 1.12 revision) until the core variant lands and this
revision then completes.

## 6. Recommendation (a recommendation only — I do not adopt it)

Placed by the DAP as a **`brokkr-core` revision** (never me), committed before this sentinel+audit revision
builds under it (§5.2), following the three-commit pattern the core Rev 1.12 revision already began.

- **(Preferred, additive) Add two distinct `ResolveError` variants**, mirroring the `SignatureInvalid`
  addition:
  - `Expired` — *"the resolution decision has expired (OQGF-P-8.5)"* (returned when `now > expiry`).
  - `ReplayedNonce` — *"the resolution decision's nonce was already accepted (OQGF-P-8.5)"* (returned when
    the nonce was already accepted for that escalation).

  **Two variants, not one combined `Stale`:** a forensic reader distinguishes a *replay* (an attack — the
  same signed decision presented twice) from an *expiry* (which may be benign latency), the same reason
  `SignatureInvalid` is distinct from `OutOfScope`. Reporting the true, specific reason is the whole point
  of Rev 1.12's `ToleranceError` correction, applied to the sibling enum.

- **(Alternative) One combined variant** (e.g. `Stale`) covering both. Simpler, but it collapses the
  attack/latency distinction a reader would want, so it is the weaker option.

**Labeling per §4 (does the recommendation unblock me?): yes — adding the variants unblocks Task 2, and I
say so plainly.** The unblock is intrinsic: the requirement is to *refuse* with an `Err(ResolveError)`, the
return type admits only `ResolveError`, and no honest refusal is possible without a fitting variant — so any
disposition that lets me proceed is one that adds the variant(s). My genuine preference is the two-variant
option because it names the two conditions truthfully, which is the same judgment that made
`SignatureInvalid` its own variant; I would prefer it even though the combined variant is less work. I adopt
neither. The choice is the DAP's, placed as a core revision.

## 7. What is ready the moment this is disposed

Adopt core's `resolution_signed_content` (delete the crate-local encoding + tag), return the new variants on
`now > expiry` and on a replayed nonce (per-escalation accepted-nonce state held in `EscalationState`),

and add the four Task-5 tests — all designed and determinable. Only the `ResolveError` variant(s) this gap
asks the DAP to place stand between here and a green workspace with freshness genuinely enforced.
