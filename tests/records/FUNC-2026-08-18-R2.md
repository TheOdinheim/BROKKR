## The load-bearing change — the test that could not fail is now the test that proves it

| Test | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| **`test_oqgf_m_14_i13_expiry_is_evaluated_against_call_time_not_a_held_clock`** (was `test_oqgf_m_14_expired_chain_is_anergy`) | built `Sindri::new(reg, Timestamp(20_000))` — `now` fixed at construction, `> expiry` — and evaluated **once**; **could not fail on a held clock** because it shared the defect's assumption | **one** `Sindri` instance evaluates the **same** chain (expiry 10 000) **twice**: `now = 500` → **Granted**; `now = 20 000` → **`ChainExpired`**. A held clock yields one verdict per chain per instance, so two verdicts from one instance is **impossible** under the pre-I-13 design — this test cannot pass against a stored clock |

**Why it catches the defect the old one could not:** the decisive property is *two different
verdicts from one instance*. Injecting `now` at construction (the old shape) can never produce that;
supplying `now` at the call (the new shape) does. Fixing the signature alone would not have changed
the old test — the test itself had to gain a way to fail.
