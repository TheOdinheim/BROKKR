## 1. What this revision does

Rev 1.6 places what a Phase-6 `Barrier::evaluate` needs to *decide*, and reshapes `BoundaryFlow` so
the decision is expressible in both directions. **Types only** — no evaluation logic, no gate, no
resolver. Scope-locked to `brokkr-core`.

- **`ids.rs`:** adds `OriginId` (the BCR provenance root).
- **`barrier.rs`:** adds `DestinationClass`, `ContextClass`, `BoundaryCustodyRecord`; **reshapes
  `BoundaryFlow`** from an egress-shaped struct into a `Egress | Ingress` sum type; adds `now:
  Timestamp` to the `Barrier::evaluate` contract.
- **`lib.rs`:** re-exports the three new barrier types.

## 3. The reshape, and why it is a sum type (Task 3)

Rev 1.5's `BoundaryFlow` carried a classification and a destination — an egress shape. It could not
express an ingress crossing at all, which made `BarrierVerdict::Quarantine { datum }` **unreachable
from `evaluate`**: the method held no `DatumRef` to construct one with, and no provenance to judge.
The sum type makes an ingress flow carrying a `Destination`, or an egress flow carrying a
`ContextClass`, **unrepresentable** — the bad shapes cannot be built. `Destination` is **unchanged**
(reasoner.rs and the negative tests use it).

## 4. The coarse `DestinationClass`, and why (Task 2)

`DestinationClass { LocalPath, Network{host}, Reasoner{endpoint} }` is deliberately coarser than
`Destination`: `Destination::Network` carries a live `ChannelStrength` and `Destination::Reasoner` a
negotiated `NamedGroup` — facts established at connection time. A BCR is signed *before* the crossing
and cannot know them; if it could pre-authorize a `ChannelStrength`, a record written when a strong
channel was available would authorize a later crossing over a classical one. "Is this destination
authorized" (the BCR) and "is this channel strong enough" (`effective_authorization`) stay separate.

## 6. Re-export convention (Task 5)

`lib.rs` gains `BoundaryCustodyRecord, ContextClass, DestinationClass` on the barrier re-export line.
**`OriginId` is not added to any ids re-export:** the convention is that `string_id!` types
(`DatumRef`, `Host`, `ModelEndpointId`, `RiskAcceptanceId`, …) are **not** re-exported at crate root —
they are reached via `brokkr_core::ids::X`. `OriginId` is a `string_id!` type, so it follows suit;
`BoundaryCustodyRecord` (which uses it) is re-exported, so consumers get the record without needing
`OriginId` re-exported. Matched the convention rather than assuming.
