## 2. The core-vs-sentinel encoding comparison (Task 1's whole point), reported

| | core `resolution_signed_content` | sentinel-local (Phase 8) |
|---|---|---|
| domain tag | `brokkr-core:resolution:v1` | `brokkr-sentinel:resolution:v1` |
| fields | `esc, cleared, dap(name,id), at, nonce, expiry` | `esc, cleared, dap(name,id), at` |

**They disagree on two counts: the tag, and the two missing fields** (`nonce`/`expiry` did not exist at
Phase 8). For the same decision they produce **different bytes**. Phase 8 was internally consistent (the
sentinel signed *and* verified with its own encoding), but a DAP tool signing via core would now be rejected
by EIR — the "field-order disagreement rejects legitimate decisions while looking exactly like an attack"
hazard §6.8 names. **This is exactly what Task 1 exists to close**, and it is buildable: call core's
function, delete the crate-local one. It is not the blocker.

## 3. Why the build stopped — `ResolveError` cannot express a freshness/replay refusal (GAP-2026-08-12-001)

§6.8 (Rev 1.12): *"EIR SHALL refuse a decision whose `now > expiry`, and SHALL refuse a `nonce` it has
already accepted for that escalation."* `resolve` returns `Result<_, ResolveError>`, so a **refusal is an
`Err(ResolveError)`** — and the three committed variants are `CriteriaNotMet` (clear condition, P-8.1),
`HysteresisNotSatisfied` (dwell/hold, P-8.3), and `NeedsDapConfirmation` (missing DAP, P-8.5). **None
represents "expired" or "replayed."**

Reusing any would misreport the refusal — most damagingly `NeedsDapConfirmation`, since a replayed decision
**was** DAP-confirmed (it carries a valid DAP signature); it is *replayed*, not *unconfirmed*. This is the
**exact** imprecision Rev 1.12 corrected for `ToleranceError` by adding `SignatureInvalid`: *"fail-closed,
but untrue in the record, in exactly the place someone investigating an attack would be reading."* Rev 1.12
added `SignatureInvalid` but **did not** add the symmetric `ResolveError` variants — the asymmetry this
Step-0 check surfaces. **Task 2 names the STOP condition verbatim**, and the fix (new `ResolveError`
variants) is a `brokkr-core` change, out of this revision's scope. I did not reuse a wrong variant, did not
write a refusal that isn't a refusal, and did not touch `brokkr-core`.

## 5. Why the whole revision stops, not a partial build

Building Tasks 1/3/4 + the arity fix would make the workspace **compile and go green** while `nonce`/
`expiry` are present and signed over but **not enforced** — a green build silently lacking the replay
protection the fields exist for, in the one act that *lowers* a defense. Task 5's
`test_oqgf_p_8_5_replayed_nonce_is_refused` — *"THE REPLAY TEST — this is why the fields exist"* — cannot
pass without Task 2. A revision that shipped unenforced freshness and could not write its own load-bearing
test would be a false completion. So it stops as a whole, pending the core variant, exactly as Phases
4/5/6/7 stopped at Step 0 on a missing core surface.

## 6. Recommendation (a recommendation only — I do not adopt it)

Placed by the DAP as a **`brokkr-core` revision** (three-commit pattern, before this revision builds under
it): add two `ResolveError` variants mirroring the `SignatureInvalid` addition — **`Expired`**
(*"the resolution decision has expired (OQGF-P-8.5)"*) and **`ReplayedNonce`** (*"the resolution decision's
nonce was already accepted (OQGF-P-8.5)"*). Two distinct variants, not one combined `Stale`, because a
forensic reader distinguishes a replay (an attack) from an expiry (possibly benign latency) — the same
judgment that made `SignatureInvalid` distinct from `OutOfScope`.

**Labeling per §4:** the recommendation **unblocks me**, and the unblock is intrinsic — the requirement is
to *refuse* with an `Err(ResolveError)`, so any disposition that lets me proceed adds a fitting variant. My
genuine preference is the two-variant option because it names the two conditions truthfully. I adopt
neither; the choice is the DAP's.
