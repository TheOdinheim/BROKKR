crate-local `pub fn resolution_signed_content`, the `const DOMAIN_RESOLUTION =
b"brokkr-sentinel:resolution:v1"`, the now-unused `use brokkr_core::resolution::ResolutionDecision`, and the

a field-order disagreement is no longer possible, only impossible. Guarded by
`test_sentinel_and_core_encodings_agree`.

**Task 2 — sentinel enforces freshness.** `EIR::resolve` now refuses an expired decision with
`ResolveError::Expired` and a replayed nonce with `ResolveError::ReplayedNonce` (see §3 for the state and
ordering).

**Task 3 — sentinel returns the true tolerance error.** `heimdall::grant_heuristic`'s signature check now
returns `ToleranceError::SignatureInvalid` (was `OutOfScope`). Order **signature → scope → expiry**,
first-failing returned: a forged grant reports `SignatureInvalid` before any scope/expiry verdict is
evaluated. Guarded by `test_oqgf_p_4_forged_grant_reports_signature_invalid`.

**Task 4 — audit records the freshness fields.** `brokkr-audit`'s `write_resolution` now appends `d.nonce.0`
and `d.expiry.0` in struct field order (between `at` and the signature), so two decisions differing only in
freshness no longer encode identically in the audit chain. **No audit test depended on those bytes** — audit
tests sign-then-verify over the encoding (self-consistent), and none hardcodes a resolution byte string; all
13 still pass. The audit **record** domain tag (`brokkr-audit:record:v1`) is unchanged, so four-crate domain
separation is unaffected (confirmed, not assumed).

## 3. The three Step-0 design decisions the prompt required, resolved and reported

**(Task 2) Nonce tracking — what is held, where, and its lifetime across resolve/re-raise.** A
`Vec<(EscalationId, Nonce)>` at the **engine level** (`EirState.accepted_nonces`), **not** on an
`EscalationState`. A nonce is recorded **only when a decision successfully resolves**, and it is **never
removed** — so:

- **It persists across resolution and re-raising.** A nonce accepted for an escalation id can never be
  reused for that id, even after the escalation stands down and is raised again. Were the set held
  per-`EscalationState`, a re-raise would start empty and a captured stand-down could be replayed against the
  fresh escalation — exactly the hole the prompt (and §6.8) warn of. **`test_oqgf_p_8_5_replayed_nonce_is_refused`
  raises `e1`, resolves, raises `e1` again, and confirms the same decision is still refused `ReplayedNonce`.**
- **Recording only on success** leaves a decision refused for hysteresis/criteria free to be re-presented
  once its hold window elapses — its nonce was not burned by a refusal that never lowered a defence.

**(Task 2) Check order — first-failing reason.** `resolve` checks: **1. signature** (→
`NeedsDapConfirmation`), **2. replay** (→ `ReplayedNonce`), **3. expiry** (→ `Expired`), **4. escalation
active** (→ `CriteriaNotMet`), **5. readiness** (→ `HysteresisNotSatisfied` / `CriteriaNotMet`). Two
orderings are deliberate:
- **Signature first**, because an unauthenticated decision's other fields — including its `nonce` and
  `expiry` — are untrustworthy; nothing else may be believed until authenticity is established.
- **Replay before expiry**, so a decision that is *both* replayed and expired names the **attack** (a
  replayed stand-down) rather than the incidental latency. Reporting `Expired` on a replay would be the
  misdirection Rev 1.13 forbids — pointing an investigator at latency during an attack.

**(Task 3) Grant check order:** signature → scope → expiry, first-failing (§2, Task 3 above).
